Architecture reference

How Vis fits together

Vis is a multi-tenant appointment-booking platform for the Georgian market. It has no application server: the React client talks straight to Supabase, and every piece of server-side logic lives either inside Postgres or in one of eleven Deno edge functions. These diagrams start with what the product actually does, then trace how it is built — outward from the browser, down to the schema, and back out through the work that runs on a schedule.

Verified against the live project on 2026-08-31 — tables, functions, cron jobs and triggers read from the running system

01What the app does

Before the wiring: the product surface, grouped by who it serves. Everything drawn here is built and shipped. Two capabilities are conditional rather than universal — phone verification and outgoing messages happen only for a business that has bought the SMS add-on, which is off by default, and manual approval is opt-in and applies to on-site bookings only.

Worth knowing

  • The customer never makes an account. A booking needs a name and a phone number, and that same phone is what later proves identity for a reschedule, a cancellation or a review.
  • Bookings are neither owner-only nor customer-only. Most arrive through the public page, but the owner can also add one from the calendar. The two are distinguished at insert, because they bill differently.
  • Money moves in two independent directions: the customer pays the business for an appointment, and separately the business pays Vis a monthly post-paid bill. They share a payment-provider seam and nothing else.
  • What is deliberately absent: no marketplace or cross-business search, no customer accounts or loyalty scheme, no recurring appointments, no public API. Several of these existed once and were removed on purpose.

02System context

Two facts shape everything below. There is no separate application server — server-side logic is RLS policies, SECURITY DEFINER functions and triggers in Postgres, plus Deno edge functions. And both external providers are mock in production today: the BOG and TBC payment adapters and the SMS provider are stubs that throw provider_not_configured. The factory fails closed on an unrecognised name rather than falling back.

Worth knowing

  • The database calls the edge functions back out over HTTP through pg_net. The dotted return edge is not decoration — SMS is dispatched by a trigger, never by the client.
  • /book/:slug is served by a Vercel edge function that rewrites an SEO block inside the SPA shell, so public booking pages are crawlable. It falls back to the untouched shell on any Supabase error and only 404s on a confirmed-missing org.

Worth knowing

  • Four functions are still deployed but dead dead — get-available-slots, book-appointment, claim-waitlist and api, the withdrawn public REST API, now a 410 tombstone. They have no local source any more but remain ACTIVE in the dashboard, pending manual deletion. They are drawn here precisely so nobody wires something to them.
  • manage-appointment calls the two OTP functions server to server and must pass x-internal-key rather than authorization — the functions gateway rewrites the authorization header on internal calls, so a bearer token would not survive the hop.
  • payment-webhook carries a deliberate dev backdoor, double-gated on ALLOW_MOCK_PAYMENTS=true. It must be unset before launch. verify-booking-otp likewise accepts the test code 000000 behind its own flags; reset-password has no such bypass, by design.
  • create-payment recomputes the amount server-side from services.price. The client's number is never trusted.

05Database

Twenty-eight live tables, every one with RLS enabled. organisations is the tenancy root — nearly everything carries an org_id back to it, and the policies are built on the get_user_org_ids() and get_user_org_role() helpers. Split into three views by domain, because twenty-eight tables on one canvas is unreadable. Columns shown are keys and the ones that carry behaviour, not the full list.

Worth knowing

  • org_members.user_id is nullable — non-login staff exist. Anything fanning out over members must tolerate that; a NOT NULL assumption here once silenced every notification for affected orgs.
  • pending_bookings is the parking lot for online bookings not yet paid for. Written and read only by the payment functions via the service role, then promoted into a real appointment by the webhook.

  • appointments.source and sms_billable are stamped by a trigger at insert, so a client cannot claim admin to dodge the per-SMS fee.

b. Billing and payments

Post-paid. Usage accrues through the month, a nightly job cuts an invoice, and a second job charges the card on file. Pricing is flat ₾15 per month plus ₾0.7 per SMS-billable appointment.

Worth knowing

  • The blocklist is checked before the OTP is sent, so a blocked number never costs an SMS.
  • claim_pending_booking exists because the original read-then-write idempotency check let a retried gateway callback both double-book and auto-refund a booking that had succeeded.
  • A pending booking does not hold its slot, so approving it re-checks capacity under the per-org advisory lock and can legitimately fail with slot_taken.
  • Anything paid online or carrying a deposit is always auto-approved — the money has already moved. Manual approval gates on-site bookings only.
  • Customers receive exactly two messages: a confirmation on approval, and a reminder the morning of.

Worth knowing

  • organisations_update has no column list. That makes trg_prevent_billing_self_update the column-level access control for the entire table — it freezes billing_status, billing_exempt, usage_anchor, owner_id and billing_review_until. Any new sensitive column on organisations is tenant-writable until it is added there.
  • trg_zz_block_blocked_customer has no service-role or superadmin bypass. It applies to every caller, deliberately.
  • reject-elapsed-pending is silent by design: the decline message fires only on a human rejection, which the trigger detects by testing auth.uid() — the cron sweep has none.
  • Other guarded tables: org_members blocks role escalation and enforces the seat cap; blocked_customers normalizes and peppers the phone on write.
